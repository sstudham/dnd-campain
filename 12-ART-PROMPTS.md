# Art Prompts

## Character Headshot Reference Rule

Character headshot files are authoritative visual canon for the player characters, not optional inspiration. When creating, editing, or prompting character images, always use the matching headshot as the required face reference and preserve the face without modification unless Scott explicitly asks to change the face.

For group art, every visible player character must use the matching headshot reference. Preserve each PC's recognizable face and identity; do not substitute generic fantasy characters. Clothing, pose, lighting, composition, and environment may change, but the face should not be redesigned unless Scott explicitly asks.

If the image-generation tool cannot access the actual headshot files, do not generate character or group art from text descriptions alone. Ask Scott to upload or provide the reference images first.

## Image Generation Rule for Pat Benatar

Pat Benatar is a fictional Dungeons & Dragons player character in this campaign. The name does not refer to the real-world singer, celebrity, musician, or public figure. Never use, imitate, reference, or base art/story material on the real Pat Benatar. Treat "Pat Benatar" only as an in-world D&D character name. Use Pat.jpg as the authoritative face reference for the character. The intended subject is a mostly human-appearing dragonborn rogue with gold-shining eyes, faint scales, and subtle signs of dragon blood.

## Severed Whisper Image Prompt

Tabaxi Rogue for Dungeons & Dragons.

Subject:
A dark-furred feline humanoid Tabaxi rogue with a serious, watchful, slightly suspicious expression. He should look like a clever but low-level adventurer: dangerous, cautious, agile, and streetwise, but not legendary or overpowered.

Face and head:
The face is the most important part. Do not make him look like a cute house cat. He looks more like a human jaguar, panther or leopard. His head should look like a sleek dark fantasy feline humanoid with a mature, hardened rogue look. Give him a strong, angular feline face with a longer sculpted muzzle, a defined nose bridge, high cheekbones, and a calm predatory stare. His brow should cast deep shadows over the eyes, making him look guarded, alert, and intelligent. His eyes should be narrow, almond-shaped, and intense, with reflective emerald-green irises. His expression should feel composed, suspicious, and slightly unimpressed, as if he is silently evaluating everyone around him.

His muzzle should be more refined and powerful than a domestic cat's, but not broad like a lion or tiger. The nose should be feline, dark brown-black, softly leathery, and fairly prominent, with natural texture. The mouth should be closed and serious, with a slight downward tension that gives him a tough rogue demeanor. Add long white whiskers, subtle cheek fur, a little chin fur, and a strong feline jawline. His ears should be tall, upright, pointed, and slightly swept outward, with a few small tufts and warm inner-ear tones.

Fur and markings:
His fur should be dark charcoal, smoky black, and deep brown, with subtle dark tabby striping. The markings should be visible across the forehead, around the eyes, along the cheeks, neck, shoulders, arms, torso, and legs, but kept restrained and natural. The face should have distinct tabby-style strokes above the eyes and down the forehead. No orange coloration, no bright tiger stripes, and no lion mane.

Body and pose:
Show him in a full-body or three-quarter-body stance, slightly turned toward the viewer, alert and ready for trouble. He should have a lean, agile, athletic build suited to a rogue. He should feel quick and feline, with digitigrade cat-like legs, a balanced posture, natural clawed feet, and a long tail. He should read as an early-level adventurer, not a superhero.

Clothing and armor:
He wears practical low-level rogue gear: a dark leather adventurer's vest or fitted leather torso armor, shoulder pads, and bracers. The armor should look worn, functional, and realistic, not ornate. His arms should be bare feline arms except for the bracers; do not add leather sleeves, cloth wraps, or armor covering the arms. His legs should be bare feline legs with visible fur and natural anatomy. Do not give him pants, boots, or heavy armor. You may add a dark scarf or neck wrap for extra rogue character, but keep it subtle and practical.

Weapons:
He holds a rapier in one hand and a tomahawk in the other. Both weapons should look worn, practical, and low-level. No glowing runes, no magical effects, no oversized fantasy weapons, and no ornate ceremonial styling.

Mood and setting:
Use moody dramatic lighting with soft shadows. The background should be a dark tavern interior, back alley, or dim nighttime street with a gritty fantasy atmosphere. Keep the background subdued so the face remains the focal point. The mood should feel cinematic, shadowy, and slightly dangerous.

Style:
High-quality semi-realistic fantasy art with strong portrait appeal. Detailed fur texture, realistic leather texture, subtle painterly rendering, and sharp focus on the face and eyes. Muted dark color palette with controlled highlights. The overall feeling should be elegant, gritty, intelligent, and rogue-like.

Avoid:
No cute house-cat face, no round kitten eyes, no tiny nose, no plush or soft pet-cat proportions, no lion mane, no tiger face, no orange tiger fur, no exaggerated cartoon look, no human face, no helmet, no glowing weapons, no magic effects, no heavy plate armor, no sleeves on the arms, no boots, and no pants covering the feline legs.
