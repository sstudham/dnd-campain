## Overall assessment
Session 17 succeeded at player agency, rescue, reconnaissance and a collaborative defense puzzle. Its weakness was the connection between those activities and the campaign's larger promise. The party learned how a raid worked; they received less usable evidence of who wanted it, what the town risked beyond the object, and why this continued their Stormwreck journey. The first Session 18 packet improved tactical resolution but continued that imbalance: much logistics, a measuring object, then another transfer marker. Scott's September 30 concern is justified. Adding cosmic vocabulary would not solve it. We need visible people, factions and consequences.

This was a setup session, not a failed combat session. The table explicitly deferred battle. No objective measure of everyone's enjoyment is available. Social banter is part of this campaign's purpose and should not be counted automatically as wasted time.

## What planning did well
- The missed delivery led to injured Doss, then tools, dispatch, camp and council. Each step had an immediate reason. Floyd GoldSeeker's rescue competence mattered before the lore.
- Wheel, Lantern and Spade supplied complementary evidence. Players could discover the operation without winning a mandatory fight or knowing a villain's name.
- A small camp with working roles supported bargaining, investigation and theft. The regional map gave the clues usable spatial context.
- Nighthill and Escobert offered bounded forces rather than unlimited soldiers. Planning became a meaningful allocation problem.
- Prepared display art and read-alouds made changing locations tangible. Saved final deployment and exact closing narration now support a reliable restart.
- Preserving Frulam and Lennithon as separate interests was a strong background choice. Preserving the mill fault as older than Stormwreck avoided making the last victory secretly harmful.

## What execution did well
- Floyd GoldSeeker helped rescue Doss; the party eventually returned Doss for care. This grounds the heroic stakes in a person they actually met.
- The party gained all three clue papers, read the conditional signal and identified the diversion. The log records actual understanding, not merely possession.
- Honest persuasion after a failed cover story was allowed to change the interaction. Camp combat was avoidable.
- Severed Whisper used the flyover distraction to pocket the map. A dramatic event became an opportunity for agency.
- The council heard evidence and invited the players' own orders. The DM did not simply deliver a compulsory battle plan.
- Scott corrected Mara's concern, the two guard TEAMS, and final positions promptly. Those corrections are preserved.
- Ending before the attack protected the players' ability to play it next time. The session did not silently resolve the battle in an epilogue.

## RAW identity versus campaign adaptation
Official Hoard opening material features Greenest defense, a deceptive mill threat, prisoners for intelligence, camp investigation and Leosin. Lennithon is a reluctant raiding ally. Our bespoke version reorders these functions and changes the mill target; it is an adaptation, not RAW encounter execution. Source: [Wizards official opening adventure](https://media.wizards.com/downloads/dnd/HoardDragonQueen_Encounters.pdf), Episodes 1-2.

Keep those recognizable functions: defend a food-producing mill, learn the raider faction through evidence, distinguish hired workers from committed cultists, preserve a camp/prisoner/hatchery trail. Do not import an adult-blue breath attack, mandatory duel, exact published geography or book-sized enemy wave into five level-2 PCs' split encounter. Greenest's approved relocation and earlier rescue stand.

Published story should supply an active antagonist and a next investigative lead. Homebrew should make the same incident mean something personal and strange. Neither layer needs to replace the other.

## Rebuilt Session 18 in six beats
1. Confirm opening orders and resources; begin immediately with drums. Preserve the actual deployments.
2. Resolve the false light and interrupted raid. Good coordination can prevent Spade entirely; no replacement ambush to erase success.
3. Regroup with one earned Cult of the Dragon dispatch and people needing care. Offer a live witness or paper backup.
4. Open the mill side recess safely. Recover a portable pressure witness; observe an old stable reading against the plate's current disturbed reading.
5. Trace the local sanctuary load, arrange care with Merra/Eadyan, choose a reversible isolation or monitored continuation. This is the homebrew moral decision. Removal is not a required price for excavation, and local repair remains incomplete.
6. Head north by earned directions with both objects if chosen. An independent dragon offers a limited explanation and a negotiated inspection. Cult route toward captives/eggs remains a distinct optional next lead.

## Pacing and acceptance checks
Three hours: 10 restart; 50 operation; 15 intelligence/aftermath; 35 mill; 25 sanctuary decision; 35 north/dragon; 10 closing. Cut travel filler and extra combats before cutting a player choice. If operation overruns, move dragon conversation to next session and retain a meaningful northbound ending.

Before the break, every PC should have made an observable decision. Before departure, players should be able to say: who ran the raid, why the mill matters, what the relief stone costs, why they want a dragon's answers, and what remains unresolved. Do not require memorizing Frulam's spelling or technical instrument terminology.

At close record actual casualties, prisoners, team status, plate/witness carriers, receiver disposition, care plan, permanent improvement, route and promises. Future events remain unplayed. The review and rebuilt prep are saved separately from actual-play history.
