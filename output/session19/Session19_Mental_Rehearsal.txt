SESSION 19 — PRIVATE DM REHEARSAL

This is a rehearsal of possibilities, not a script for the players or a prediction of their choices.

Picture the mill at the instant the last session ended. Brother Kai holds the pink crystal beside Floyd GoldSeeker. The supports have held through the tremor. Outside the scorched barricade, the enemy commander remains. Severed Whisper is beyond him, bloodied and furious. Four friendly guards hold the next part of the approach. Farther along is a separate enemy wagon, with Throk, Pat-Benatar and the rear allies closing from behind.

Begin by giving the players that clear physical picture. They do not need a summary of every guard’s journey. They need to know where help and danger are now. Severed Whisper has three hit points. The commander may also be wounded. Those existing facts provide plenty of pressure without adding a stronger enemy.

Ask each player for an immediate action. Someone may protect the workers, someone may cover Severed Whisper, and someone may call for surrender. Throk’s hawk offers a different view, but the decision remains his. Pat-Benatar has already shown what superior numbers can accomplish. Floyd can protect people through practical direction. Brother Kai must decide what to do while holding the very thing the attackers want. These are opportunities, not assigned roles.

Play the commander as a person with a failing operation. He wants the recovered object and a way out. His first crew has fled. He might bluster, bargain, fight or accept terms. There is no required final duel. The wagon escorts face a pinch between friendly forces, while their workers want shelter or safety. Resolve guard attacks in batches, with separate hit points, and keep attention on the PCs’ consequential actions.

If the party creates a credible surrender, let it work. If they keep fighting, preserve wounds and normal movement. Neither a short victory nor a negotiated ending leaves you short of session material. The next part is about what the victory is for.

After danger ends, slow the pace. The mill still carries weight on timber. Workers need to know whether they can go home. Guards need a destination for captives. Escobert needs an account he can act on. A captive wants to know whether surrender means survival. Put one practical question in front of the party at a time and let their decisions shape the scene.

An informed opponent or recovered dispatch can reveal Frulam Mondath and the Cult of the Dragon. The instructions distinguish two objectives: recover the contents through the side breach while preserving the mill and old channels, and send the intact plate north separately. The camp counts returning people and prisoners. A guarded cave holds stock. That last word does not yet mean eggs. Let the mystery stay precise.

The northern instruction gives a route the players can use: cart road beyond the camp, last chalk fork, three pale stones, then a foot track to the split basalt shelf. Do not make them replay the whole camp investigation. Their earlier work should make the next step easier.

The crystal offers a quieter discovery. If the party brings the plate close, a second amber line appears briefly, offset from the first. Give the observation without a roll. Examination can suggest a stored local reference and a present reading. This explains something they can see without explaining the whole wound in reality. The original arch stays shut and the broken branch still needs repair.

Then connect the old mechanism to ordinary lives. A sanctuary-facing channel can pulse and shed grit. Someone who has seen Merra’s relief stone can recognize the timing. That gives the party a reason to investigate the sanctuary, while leaving the northern route available if they choose it first.

At the sanctuary, begin with patients receiving care. Merra is sincere and protective of what helps them. Eadyan wants dependable treatment and visible evidence. They can disagree without either being a villain. Their shared purpose is to keep people breathing.

Let the players watch a full cycle. Breathing eases when the pale receiver pulses; grit falls at the damaged support. The connection is visible. A failed check should not erase it. The meaningful question is how to keep helping without adding hidden strain.

A complete care plan can work through fresh air, water, bandages, helpers and a suitable place. Merra can agree to a supervised lift onto an ordinary wooden bench. Relief and extra pulsing stop together, while ordinary care keeps the patients stable. Alternatively, the party can brace the vulnerable support and leave the stone in monitored use with named watchers and a review time.

Either responsible choice changes what happens after the party leaves. The wider fault remains, but the improvement is real. Do not require a verdict about religion or force a patient sacrifice. Let the characters’ priorities emerge in how they organize help.

This is enough to occupy a substantial part of the evening. If the confrontation and town decisions fill the session, close with a concrete clue, a responsible handoff and the chosen next objective. You do not need to rush a dragon conversation to make the preparation worthwhile.

If the party leaves, confirm actual healing or rest rather than quietly refilling resources. Ask who carries the plate and crystal and what question each character most wants answered. Move along the earned route and give the journey a different rhythm from the crowded town.

At the chalk fork, the pale stones lead to the narrower track. The shelf provides an approach and a visible way back. Let the hawk scout, the party hail from a distance, or the group choose concealment. Good preparation changes the opening of the meeting. It should not simply trigger an unavoidable surprise fight.

Lennithon’s power is obvious, but his specific interest makes the scene playable. He wants the local baseline and the plate interface compared with northern disturbances. He prefers possession, but supervised inspection and copied observations can also serve him. That gives the party something to negotiate.

Let him answer an actual question before making another demand. He can distinguish the old physical fault at Greenest from the outward change felt after Stormwreck. He can explain the crystal’s limited reference function. He can clarify that his interests are independent of Frulam’s. One useful answer makes a bargain credible.

The party may keep both objects throughout a test, exchange copies of observations, ask for answers first, or discuss temporary custody with a duration and a return location. They may refuse. A refusal can end cooperation without becoming a breath weapon. This is a meeting with an independent actor, not a balanced combat encounter for level-two characters.

Keep his knowledge bounded. He does not settle every dragon sighting, Throk’s patron, Pat-Benatar’s ancestry or the ultimate cause of the wound. He can still change the campaign by making one connection clear and offering a choice with real terms.

Reserve ten minutes for the ending. The players may follow Frulam’s camp and cave lead, return with readings to help Greenest, or accept a limited northern observation. Ask which matters most now. Record the actual casualties, captives, resources, item carriers, care arrangements, knowledge and promises. The next session begins from those choices. The unused branches remain preparation.
