Before Greenest, before the dragon at the Observatory, there was a warning.

In the silence of a monastery, your minds were drawn beyond the world you knew. Something immense reached across realities and spoke through the shape of an ancient spellcaster. Time, space, magic, prayer, even memory were beginning to fray. The wound was larger than one town, one cult, or one god. And the being powerful enough to summon you did not know what was causing it.

Since then, you have been following pieces of a problem too large to see all at once.

On Stormwreck Isle, you found sickness in the caves, restless death aboard the Compass Rose, and a dragon trying to draw power from the dead beneath an ancient Observatory. You also found people who needed you. Some you saved. Some you could only bring home.

At the Observatory, Pat's arrow struck Sparkrender hard. Throk's blast finished him. But killing the dragon did not finish the work. The tower shuddered, the old pattern broke loose, and you had to understand what stood around you before it destroyed what lay below.

Together, you solved the Dragon of Dawn puzzle and opened the way down. You freed Aidron, the bronze dragon imprisoned beneath the tower. Then you returned to Dragon's Rest with one dragon alive and one small kobold dead. Over Mek's body, Minn said only, "He came home."

Pat learned that the mark on her scale belonged to something far older than her clan. Floyd carried a question of his own: why had he survived Glittervein when his crew had not? At the Observatory, he had heard the words, "You were not the only one buried. Dig."

Runara gave you a direction. If you wanted to understand the wound, follow the dragons. Aidron warned you that this did not mean trusting them.

As Stormwreck fell behind your ship, Pat's scale warmed. A golden point moved beyond the horizon. Something vast and winged passed behind it, then vanished. You still do not know what you saw.

Greenest put faces to the danger again.

Raiders were carrying away a fitted plate from beneath the mill. You stopped them. Sev ran one down and held him. But nearby, a worker lay trapped beneath damaged timbers, and the questions about stolen machinery had to wait.

Pat brought the volunteers together. Floyd worked out how to support the weight, with Sev helping him make the rig hold. Throk pulled the worker free, and Floyd gave him healing. You brought him to the sanctuary alive.

There, Eadyan and Sister Merra cared for the wounded. A pale stone pulsed beside the patients. Their breathing eased. Dust fell near a crack in the stairs. You saw the connection, but you have not yet learned what it means.

Your search took you to a map in the town library, then to a tavern, some questionable drinks, and an even more questionable monkey guide. Whatever Boots seemed to be doing, it was your own knowledge of the map that brought you through the caverns.

Beneath the mill, you found the old arch: dark stone, brass channels, and an empty socket that fitted the plate in Throk's hands.

You put it back.

Amber light travelled through the channels. Water moved where water should not move. Then the pulse reached a break in the masonry, faltered, and died. Again and again, the same interruption. The arch stayed closed. Replacing one missing part could not mend everything that had been damaged.

So you removed the plate and chose to follow the people who wanted it.

Their instructions were simple. Under the mill. Plate intact. Do not turn the channels. North road, then the chalk cut. Deliver before dawn.

Now dawn is here.

Behind you are the worker you rescued, the patients in the sanctuary, and a town built above machinery somebody has been taking apart. Ahead is the person expecting its missing piece. They may know what it does. They may know why a dragon matters. At the very least, they know something they did not put on that paper.

The road narrows between raw white walls. Fresh wheel tracks curve out of sight. Throk carries the dark stone plate, its brass markings catching the first light.

Around the bend, somebody shouts, "Stop pulling. You're making it worse!"

A mule cart stands beside a fresh collapse. A young man lies pinned under chalk and broken timber. A guard strains against a lever. The woman beside them turns toward your footsteps.

Her eyes move from your faces to the plate.

"You're late."
