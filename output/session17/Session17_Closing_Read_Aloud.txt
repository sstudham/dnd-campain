Escobert looks down at the map one last time.

Two guard teams for Pat-Benatar. The third for Throk. Both volunteer work crews for the mill.

His finger rests on the north road.

“And you?”

Severed Whisper’s expression suggests that the question answers itself.

Escobert studies him for a moment, then turns to the waiting messengers. Chairs scrape across the floor. Orders pass from one person to another. Outside, Greenest carries on with the ordinary business of an afternoon, while the people in this room prepare for dusk.

At the mill, Floyd GoldSeeker faces the stonework again. This time, there are hands enough to carry timber, lay out ropes, and bring the tools he needs. Two crews wait for his direction. They are townspeople who have agreed to help, and every one of them will depend on his judgment once the work begins.

Brother Kai stands beside him. Beyond the familiar masonry, the sealed space keeps its secret. The enemy intends to come for whatever lies inside. Floyd GoldSeeker and Brother Kai intend to reach it first.

Farther north, Pat-Benatar leads two guard teams toward the signal ridge. From that height, the enemy’s observers expect to watch the town answer the drums, then release the hidden movement with a single long flash.

Pat-Benatar has another signal in mind.

First, she must reach the observers. Then she must take their position. Only after that can she put her wager to the test: whether a false light can turn the enemy’s carefully arranged instructions against them.

On the west path, Throk takes his place with the remaining guard team. The guards look to him, then along the track ahead. Here, away from the mill and the ridge, the afternoon sounds quieter. They settle into position and wait to see what comes their way.

And on the north road, Severed Whisper stands alone.

The road stretches toward the chalk hills. Somewhere beyond them, wagons are being prepared, drums are waiting, and people believe they know how Greenest will respond.

The tabaxi watches the empty approach with extraordinary confidence.

Across the town, the distance between companions grows. A voice that could have reached everyone around the council table will now need a runner, a signal, or precious minutes of travel.

The light begins to soften. Shadows lengthen across the road. At the mill, tools lie ready. On the ridge, Pat-Benatar watches the ground ahead. Along the west path, Throk and his guards hold their position.

You have read the enemy’s orders.

You have chosen your own.

Now dusk is coming.

And that is where we leave you.
