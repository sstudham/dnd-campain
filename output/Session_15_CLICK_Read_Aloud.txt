SESSION 15 - CLICK MECHANIC READ-ALOUD

READ BEFORE PLAY

Before we begin, I am adding a new table mechanic called CLICK.

Most of the time, I will describe rooms, passages, and dangers exactly as I always have. But every few sessions, you may hear me suddenly say the word CLICK, loudly and by itself.

When you hear CLICK, you have three real-world seconds to say one short, immediate thing your character does. Run. Duck. Jump. Grab someone. Raise a shield. Cast something. Freeze. Whatever your first instinct is.

You do not need to name a rule, choose a skill, or roll a die during those three seconds. Just tell me your character's immediate intention. You cannot confer with the group, ask me to repeat the description, search your character sheet, or change your answer after hearing somebody else's. If several people speak at once, that is fine. Hold on to your answer and I will repeat everyone's choices back before resolving them.

If you do not answer within three seconds, your character hesitates or freezes for that instant.

Once the three seconds are over, normal game time resumes. I will resolve what everyone attempted, call for rolls when the outcome is uncertain, and let creative ideas work when they fit the situation.

CLICK is not meant to be a random gotcha. Before I use it, there will be at least one clue in the description that can help you recognize the danger. There may be several good answers; you are not trying to guess one magic phrase. The point is to reward attention and capture the split-second instincts of an adventurer when there is no time for a committee meeting.

Only a loud CLICK said by itself activates this rule. Tonight's first one is an introduction so we can learn how it feels.


LATER - READ WHEN THE D5 STAIRCASE OPENS

The center of the star map sinks, and the newly opened stairs coil beneath the Observatory.

After a dozen steps, the stairs flatten into a passage unlike the chambers above. The walls curve inward until you are walking through the inside of a smooth stone cylinder. The entire passage slopes steeply downhill. Long scrape marks run along its center, all pointing toward the darkness below. Shallow maintenance recesses interrupt the rounded walls, and somewhere behind you, stone grinds slowly against stone as though a great weight has shifted in its cradle.

You step forward.

CLICK.
