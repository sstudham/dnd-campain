## The mill rescue

Use art 02 and the original mill map • About forty minutes

THE HUMAN STAKE: A conscious adult is pinned in a survivable air pocket. The mill can be abandoned. The person can be saved. The party decides how much structure and evidence to preserve.

## Show the facts before asking for a roll

The waterwheel is still turning. Its shaft shakes a split support. A floor opening exposes the trapped worker below. Flour dust drifts toward a second vent, showing another route. Rope, wedges and two stout spare beams are visible. A cart and four willing adults wait outside.

Floyd recognizes automatically that lifting the central beam first would transfer weight onto the air pocket. He can see two workable approaches: stop the wheel and brace the load, or reach the pocket through the downstream vent. Give these facts to Andy; ask whom he wants to involve. Other credible plans work.

## Run three objectives rather than a success counter

- 1. Stop the movement. Secure the external water gate, disengage the drive or tie off the wheel safely. The accessible gate and two helpers work without a check. A rushed improvised maneuver uses DC 10 Athletics or suitable tools.

- 2. Preserve breathing room. Wedge and brace the load, or clear the narrow vent and bring supports through it. A sound plan with tools and help works. An uncertain lift or cramped entry uses DC 11 Athletics, Acrobatics or an appropriate tool check. Use the ability that fits the described work.

- 3. Free and move the worker. With the load supported, remove the obstruction and use a blanket as a sling. No roll is needed for a controlled extraction. A hasty extraction while supports remain uncertain uses DC 11 with the consequence stated first.

## Consequences are bounded and visible

A failed uncertain action marks one setback and changes the situation: first, splintering closes the easiest approach; second, slipping debris threatens the acting PC (DC 10 Dexterity save, 1d4 bludgeoning on failure, zero on success); third, the main floor settles and the crew must finish through the clearly visible vent. The rescue remains possible, but the mill needs major repair. Do not repeat the damage event; total rescue hazard damage is at most 1d4 per PC.

If the party ignores a described imminent fall or chooses a dangerous shortcut, warn of the specific consequence before resolving it. Do not make the rescue harder because a spell, rope or clever plan makes it easy. The worker is alive, stable but trapped; no death-save countdown is running. Healing can improve his condition but does not move the beam.

## Invite contributions without assigning them

Pat can locate the vent and notice the old seam. Sev can reach the gate, crawl a safe passage or rig rope. Kai can read the worker’s breathing and coordinate timing. Throk can hold a brace, direct volunteers or move the cart. Floyd can survey and teach. Ask “What do you do?” rather than requiring these roles. Use player-declared resources; do not assume unverified spells.

## The approved local effect

The pale stone rests above a buried branch channel. While seated there, within 20 feet it eases pain and labored breathing for three injured, conscious civilians. This is a narrow story effect: it grants no hit points, temp HP, spell slots, immunity or general PC buff. Ordinary treatment and moving patients away from smoke can replace its immediate benefit.

Every ten fictional minutes it pulses faintly. At that moment the patients relax and dust falls from a crack beside the cellar stair. Start with one witnessed pulse. The stone is routing power or prayer through a non-divine receiver; this is consistent with the earlier worshiping-stone concern, not proof that the One God is real. Merra’s belief is her interpretation.

## A safe first experiment

- Explain the visible correlation automatically to anyone watching a full cycle. Medicine, Arcana or Investigation DC 11 adds useful corroboration: symptoms respond in time with the pulse, or the crack widens at the same instant. Failure costs ten minutes; it does not conceal what everyone can see.

- Merra agrees to a brief removal once Eadyan and two helpers have fresh air, water, bandages and a place for the patients. Help arrange that care; no Persuasion roll is required for a complete, respectful proposal.

- Lift the stone onto an ordinary wooden bench away from the channel for one cycle. The pulse, new dust and pain relief stop together. Patients remain stable with care. Replacing it restores the paired effect. This demonstrates a link, not the full mechanism or an ultimate villain.

## If they choose quickly

Remove it: relief stops, replacement care continues, and Merra asks to help investigate. Leave it in place: mark the measured risk, shore up the stair and invite further testing in Session 17. Break it: local pulsing stops, its potential use is lost, and frightened dependents demand an explanation; no explosion or instant deaths. Confiscate it roughly: cooperation deteriorates, but the healer still protects patients and tells the truth.

A temporary prop buys at least one night of safety at the sanctuary; tonight has no secret instant-collapse clock. The wider damaged vault remains a repair problem whichever choice they make. Removing this receiver reduces one load but does not magically repair the foundations.

## Opening  Smoke over Greenest

Stormwreck is behind you.

You left a living dragon beside Runara, and you brought Mek home. The Observatory’s terrible insistence has ended. Whatever else is wrong with the world, that much is true. You changed something. You saved someone.

But the sea did not give you an answer to the light beyond the horizon. Pat’s scale cooled. The shadow passed. Runara’s advice remained: follow the dragons.

Days later, on the mainland, the rumors become more specific. A dragon has been seen above a market town called Greenest. You take the inland road. By late afternoon, the smell reaches you before the town does: wet ash, scorched grain, and smoke.

From the ridge, you can see a stone keep above a cluster of roofs. A stream bends past a watermill. The bell tower is ringing too quickly. Lanterns move through the streets in little knots as people help one another toward shelter.

Far above the smoke, something broad-winged crosses a break in the clouds. For a moment it holds there. Then the clouds close around it. You cannot make out its color.

Below, two figures struggle with a crate in a narrow lane. Elsewhere, neighbors form a line and pass buckets toward a burning roof. At the mill, people are trying to pull a timber free. They stop together when the building gives a low, grinding groan.

A woman runs toward the road, one sleeve torn and white with flour. She looks from your weapons to the tools you carry.

“There’s a man under the mill floor. He’s answering us. Every time we pull a beam, another one moves.”

She swallows, glances back, and points.

“Please. If you know how to get him out, come now.”

## At the mill  When they enter

The wheel is still turning. Each rotation draws a shudder through the floor. A grinding stone rocks against its housing, and a split support ticks softly under the strain.

Through the broken boards, a voice says, “Still here.”

A face turns toward your light beneath a slanted beam. There is room around his chest, but his leg is pinned. Fine flour dust moves past him toward a narrow opening in the far wall. Beside the door lie rope, wooden wedges and two unused beams.

Under the new break in the floor, the stone is different: closer fitted, darker, and crossed by one dull line of brass.

## Floyd  Give the useful fact plainly

Floyd, you recognize how the weight is traveling. Pulling that central beam first would crush the space he is breathing in. You can stop the wheel and brace the load, or work through the vent beyond him. You can see where the first support needs to go.

What do you tell the others?
