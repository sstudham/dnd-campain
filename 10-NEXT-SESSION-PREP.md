# Next Session Prep

## Current Starting Point

Act 1, Scene 2 is complete.

The party has left Stormwreck Isle after resolving the Clifftop Observatory crisis, freeing Aidron, returning Mek's body to Dragon's Rest, and receiving Runara and Aidron's next lead: if they want to understand the wound, follow the dragons.

Pat Benatar's scale now has a stronger established connection to an ancient draconic pattern that predates her clan. After departure, the scale reacted to a distant golden light and unidentified winged shadow beyond the horizon.

The next play begins Act 1, Scene 3.

## Player Direction for Scene 3

The player planning discussion produced these priorities:

* Moral choices: 3 votes.
* Roleplay: 3 votes.
* Puzzles: 2 votes.
* Party bonding: 2 votes.
* Mystery: 5 votes and the strongest requested adventure feel.
* Also desired: rescue mission, mixed adventure, enemy-camp infiltration, and chase elements.
* The Cult of the One God should return.
* Grusk and Morghul should not return during Scene 3; preserve them for a later arc.
* Dragons should be independent actors with their own interests and alignments, not default allies or enemies.
* Scene 3 should connect directly to Stormwreck through dragons.
* Floyd GoldSeeker is the primary character-development focus for approximately the next six months of real-world play.

## Scene 3 Core Concept

Use the existing Greenest-inspired Level 3 material, but shift the center of gravity toward mystery, rescue, roleplay, moral choices, infiltration, puzzles, and party bonding rather than serial combat.

Runara and Aidron know that what happened at the Observatory reached outward and that other dragons likely sensed it. They direct the party toward mainland dragons or ancient draconic sites where more may be known about the wound.

The party expects to investigate dragons and instead arrives at a settlement under immediate threat.

The settlement should lead naturally into the Greenest-inspired material already planned.

Do not frame the principal dragon as automatically hostile, friendly, controlled, or victimized. The dragon has its own interests. Different factions may interpret its motives differently, and all may be partly wrong.

## Scene 3 Adventure Shape

Likely 4-6 session flow. Preserve player choice and adjust as play changes the situation.

### 1. Arrival / Rescue / Mystery

* Arrive at a threatened Greenest-like settlement.
* Lead with civilians in danger and practical rescue rather than endless combat encounters.
* Establish contradictory evidence about why dragons and raiders are present.
* Give Floyd an immediate structural-rescue opportunity.

### 2. Investigation / Roleplay / Puzzle 1

* Interview survivors, prisoners, cult members, witnesses, and anyone who saw the dragon clearly.
* Let accounts conflict.
* Include a puzzle revealing that foundations, tunnels, or buried architecture beneath the settlement are older than the visible town.
* Floyd's surveyor/miner knowledge should help distinguish later construction from older buried structures.

### 3. Pursuit / Chase / Moral Choice

* Raiders or another relevant force withdraws.
* Make pursuit a choice rather than an automatic next encounter.
* The party may need to choose among immediate pursuit, continued rescue, local investigation, and town defense.
* Use a chase if the players pursue aggressively.

### 4. Enemy Camp Infiltration

* Favor stealth, disguise, social play, prisoners, faction tension, and intelligence gathering over a mandatory frontal assault.
* Give Severed Whisper and Pat Benatar strong functional roles while keeping Floyd as the primary emotional arc.
* Allow negotiation or unexpected alliances if earned.

### 5. Dragon Interaction

* Build toward a major roleplay scene with an independent dragon.
* The dragon may know more about ancient draconic sites or the disturbance sensed from Stormwreck, but should not provide a complete lore dump.
* The dragon should have its own priorities and may demand something, withhold information, lie, negotiate, or simply refuse.
* Do not predetermine combat as the resolution.

### 6. Buried-Site Climax / Puzzle 2 / Moral Choice

* Use an underground or buried structure connected to the older draconic pattern.
* Include a second puzzle.
* Put trapped people, unstable structures, and important rift/draconic evidence under simultaneous pressure.
* Make the climax a meaningful choice rather than only a boss fight.
* If the players invent a solution that protects both people and evidence at meaningful cost or risk, let it work.

## Floyd GoldSeeker Spotlight

Floyd is the primary Scene 3 character-development focus.

Do not answer why Floyd survived Glittervein.
Do not define his god, domain, exact source of the golden light, or whether his survival was cosmically chosen.

The Scene should instead test what Floyd does with survival.

Core recurring question:

> Floyd survived being buried. What does he do when he keeps finding other people who have not gotten out yet?

Use Floyd's established identity as a master surveyor, miner, survivor, cleric, and protector.

Useful Floyd opportunities:

* Recognize unstable stone, hidden loads, unsafe excavation, old tunneling, air pockets, and safe digging routes.
* Organize frightened civilians into a competent rescue effort.
* Identify that buried architecture or foundations predate the current settlement.
* Notice structural clues that other characters would reasonably miss.
* Save people through practical knowledge rather than divine revelation.
* Encounter someone who survived being buried against terrible odds and let the roleplay happen without prescribing Floyd's conclusion.
* Build toward a difficult choice between trapped people and evidence, treasure, a powerful object, or a unique clue.

Theme to preserve:

* One possible interpretation is that Floyd was chosen for a purpose.
* Another is that Floyd creates purpose through what he chooses to do after surviving.
* Do not decide that question for Andy.

## Cult of the One God

Bring the Cult of the One God back during Scene 3, but do not make it the default primary antagonist.

Prefer at least one recurring sincere believer who gives the cult a human face.

A worshiping stone or similar receiver may appear to provide genuine comfort, healing, answered prayer, or communal stability while still being structurally dangerous or connected to the wound.

The party should face a real problem rather than a simple evil-object solution:

* Destroying or disabling the object may reduce structural danger.
* People may genuinely depend on it.
* Preserving it may leave an unresolved danger.
* A social, spiritual, technical, or negotiated alternative may be possible if the party creates one.

Do not introduce Acererak, Concordance terminology, the Invariant, or other non-canon campaign-planning explanations as established truth.

## Dragon Framing

Dragons are an independent force.

* Different dragons can have different motives and alignments.
* The Cult of the Dragon may think a dragon is cooperating when it is merely using them.
* The Cult of the One God may interpret dragon behavior as religious evidence.
* Townspeople may reasonably assume any dragon overhead is attacking them.
* The party should have to discover motive rather than receive it automatically.
* Negotiation with a dragon should be dangerous and meaningful without requiring combat.

## Grusk and Morghul

Do not bring Grusk or Morghul into Scene 3.

They remain active unresolved campaign hooks for a later action or arc.

## Canon Boundaries

The material in this file is preparation and approved direction, not predetermined outcomes.

Do not make any of the following canon until they occur in play:

* Exact Greenest events.
* A specific dragon's identity or motive.
* A specific worshiping stone effect.
* Any proposed trapped survivor.
* Any exact buried structure.
* Any exact Floyd moral choice or answer.
* Any camp infiltration outcome.
* Any cult alliance, betrayal, or defeat.
* Any explanation for the rift, Pat Benatar's ancestry, or Floyd's survival.
