# Campaign Structure

Planning structure, not canon unless later confirmed in canon files.

## Table Purpose and Campaign Mode

* Primary campaign mode: player-driven sandbox.
* Primary purpose: social gathering, shared storytelling, and using D&D as a tool for getting together.
* The story should support the table experience, not dominate it.
* The campaign is meant to create a shared storyline collaboratively.
* Player experience and focus varies:
  * Andy and Kim are newer players.
  * Dave has played for a while but is not highly focused on D&D mechanics or lore.
  * Mike and Erik enjoy D&D lore.
* Prep should support both casual/social play and deeper lore engagement.
* Do not assume every player is tracking complex lore, names, or factions.
* The current priority is party cohesion and shared identity before deep plot advancement.

## Table Mechanic: CLICK - Three-Second Instinct

Use the **CLICK** mechanic roughly once every two to four sessions, at irregular intervals, when an environmental clue can reward close attention.

* Only a loud, isolated **"CLICK"** from the DM activates the mechanic. Ordinary uses of the word do not count.
* Before the trigger, describe at least one fair, actionable clue about the danger or rapidly changing situation.
* When the DM says **"CLICK,"** every player has three real-world seconds to call out one short, immediate action or intention.
* Players may not confer, ask for the description again, search their sheets, or revise an action after hearing someone else's response.
* A player who gives no response within three seconds hesitates or freezes for that instant.
* The declaration does not need rules language. "Run downhill," "dive aside," "grab Pat," "raise my shield," or "freeze" is enough.
* Overlapping answers are allowed. After the three seconds, the DM repeats each declared intention back and resolves the moment using normal narration, checks, saves, and consequences.
* Reward responses that fit the clues and situation; allow creative answers rather than requiring one exact phrase.
* Use the declared action to set position, advantage, disadvantage, or a reasonable DC. An obviously effective action may work without a roll.
* CLICK should create urgency, surprise, and memorable consequences, not arbitrary instant death. Do not use it for complex moral choices, dialogue, or irreversible character decisions that deserve time to think.
* Introduce the mechanic with moderate stakes. Later uses may become more dangerous once the table understands the signal.

## Current Rift / One God Framing

* The "one god" cult currently gives a human and religious face to the rift in space, time, religion, and magic.
* The cult consists of people exploring, interpreting, or exploiting the rift.
* The players do not know the full truth.
* The "one god" is likely a false interpretation.
* The cult is not the main plotline at this stage.
* The rift remains the larger mystery, but the campaign should not currently revolve around solving it.

## Long-Term Campaign Structure

* This is a long-term D&D campaign emphasizing collaborative storytelling and collective worldbuilding in a world loosely inspired by Faerun.
* The campaign may span a decade or more of real-time play.
* The campaign is expected to include 10+ Acts.
* Each Act may last approximately one year of real-time play.
* Each Act may include 4-6 Scenes.
* Each Scene may last 3-5 Sessions.
* Character progression is intentionally slow, usually 1-3 levels per Act.
* DM duties may rotate by Scene or Act.

## Act and Scene Structure

* Acts are major plot arcs defined collaboratively before play.
* Each Act should draw on group input for major events, villains, and themes.
* Scenes are smaller arcs within Acts.
* Create high-level Scene outlines before an Act.
* Prepare detailed encounters, NPCs, and hooks shortly before play to preserve flexibility.
* At the start of each Act, ask each player whether their character's proposed pressure points still interest them.
* Players may accept, redirect, postpone, or reject any planned character arc.

## Proposed Concordance Campaign Structure

### Campaign-Level Principles

* The Concordance and Acererak material is approved non-canon planning, not established world truth.
* Acererak is the leading proposed central antagonist until the campaign group confirms that direction.
* The level-by-level spine below is a candidate route, not a railroad.
* Published adventures are a menu of useful locations, maps, encounters, NPCs, and conflicts.
* The campaign is not required to run every listed book or chapter.
* Levels and modules are approximate and may move, merge, split, or be replaced through play.
* Each chosen module must function as a complete local story.
* Players should care about immediate people and danger without remembering every cosmic term.
* The larger Concordance plot connects modules without replacing their identities.
* One major Concordance revelation per level is usually enough.
* Retuned Anchors remain repaired.
* Acererak cannot arbitrarily reset completed adventures.
* Player repairs are always a net improvement.
* Local antagonists retain independent motives.
* The final outcome should depend on accumulated relationships and choices, not collecting identical objects.

### Branching and Continuity Rules

* Every candidate module must allow refusal, failure, negotiation, or an unexpected route.
* "Continuity Payload" means information or consequences the campaign may need later; it does not dictate how players obtain or resolve them.
* If the party bypasses a candidate module, move only the necessary clue, witness, or pressure to a different location.
* Do not force a published villain, location, or ending merely because it appears in this plan.
* When an adventure ends differently than expected, preserve the player-created result and revise the later spine.
* A restored site may remain scarred, politically complicated, or culturally changed without making the restoration a mistake.

### Adaptation Effort

* Low:
  * The published level and structure closely fit the party.
  * Reframing and connective material should be enough.
* Moderate:
  * Encounters, levels, or motivations require meaningful adjustment.
  * Most maps and local structure remain usable.
* Heavy / mythic conversion:
  * The source is being used primarily for locations, architecture, NPCs, traps, or themes.
  * Encounter math and the campaign engine require substantial rebuilding.
* Reassess adaptation effort immediately before each Act.
* Heavy conversions should never be described as workload-saving modules.

## Why the Party Matters

* The player characters are not required to be prophesied champions.
* They are not necessarily descendants of the Concordance's creators.
* One possible explanation for the monastery vision is that:
  * They had encountered early planar instability.
  * They were gathered during a rare moment of multiversal openness.
  * They had made choices contrary to roles their cultures assigned them.
  * They could survive contradiction without immediately eliminating it.
* The higher-dimensional being may have reached whoever it could reach.
* The being did not fully understand why these people might become important.
* Floyd GoldSeeker joined after the monastery vision.
* Floyd's separate path shows that future party members may join through their own contact with the rift, a Relay, or a damaged Anchor.
* The campaign must survive player changes, character departures, guest characters, and rotating DMs.
* The party matters because its members repeatedly choose responsibility after learning that the problem is larger than them.

## Character Themes Versus Acererak

* Acererak believes contradiction must be eliminated until only one stable reality remains.
* The player characters contain contradictions that may produce growth:
  * Brother Kai:
    * Dragon instinct and disciplined restraint.
  * Pat Benatar:
    * Dragonborn identity and exclusion from her clan.
  * Severed Whisper:
    * Independence and increasing responsibility to others.
  * Throk:
    * Orc martial tradition and construction of a different future.
  * Floyd GoldSeeker:
    * Survival and responsibility to those who did not survive.
* Possible thematic answers:
  * Coherence does not require sameness.
  * Uncertainty does not eliminate responsibility.
  * Difference does not make a person incomplete.
  * Freedom includes protecting choices for others.
  * Purpose may be chosen rather than assigned.
* These are questions for play, not predetermined player conclusions.

## Module Design

Every major module should aim to contain:

* One local problem that works without the cosmic plot.
* No more than one major Concordance discovery.
* One primary player-character spotlight opportunity.
* One secondary character contribution.
* One party relationship moment.
* One choice capable of leaving an irreversible consequence.
* One visible later consequence.
* One clear reason to care beyond gaining a level.

Additional rules:

* Growth moments must involve player choices.
* A lore reveal is not automatically character development.
* Do not make one character mechanically indispensable to the campaign.
* Do not finalize player-owned mysteries without player participation.
* Approximately every three or four levels, offer a homeward echo involving:
  * Brother Kai's monastery or master.
  * Pat Benatar's mother, clan, or scale.
  * Severed Whisper's Luskan trouble or Captain Braddok Ironhand.
  * Throk's father Bagronk, sister Grek, Grusk, Morghul, or Many-Arrows.
  * Floyd GoldSeeker's clan, Glittervein, or families of his dead crew.
* The multiverse should grow larger without making the emotional world smaller.

## Proposed Player-Owned Character Questions

### Pat Benatar — Belonging Without Simplification

Possible pressure points:

* Would one complete explanation of her ancestry free her or confine her?
* Can she define belonging without permission from a clan or cosmic system?

Optional spotlight opportunities:

* Around Level 4:
  * Find an incomplete clan connection while living dragon eggs or captives need help.
* Around Level 8:
  * Encounter a false history in which her clan always accepted her.
* Around Level 12:
  * Help preserve two conflicting but meaningful histories.
* Around Level 15:
  * Speak before dragons and factions in her own name.
* Late campaign:
  * Encounter contradictory versions of her ancestry without being required to accept any as definitive.

### Severed Whisper — Freedom and Stewardship

Possible pressure points:

* Does freedom require always having an exit?
* Is the party worth demanding accountability from?
* Can protecting routes and choices for others expand freedom rather than limit it?

Optional spotlight opportunities:

* Levels 2-3:
  * Decide how to respond to Kai and whether the party relationship is worth repairing.
* Around Level 5:
  * Return to Luskan and decide what to share about the Harmonic Regulator or odd stone.
* Levels 9-10:
  * Learn that the odd stone may be a damaged route key.
* Around Level 17:
  * Accept, refuse, or reshape responsibility for dangerous planar routes.
* Late campaign:
  * Encounter the offer of a private route through every reality at the cost of isolation or control.

### Throk — Strength and Building a Future

Possible pressure points:

* How much does Throk need his father or people to validate his path?
* Can protection and restraint remain recognizably orcish strength?
* Can he build a future strong enough to survive disagreement?

Optional spotlight opportunities:

* Levels 3-4:
  * Confront Grusk and Morghul without reducing the choice to revenge.
* Around Level 6:
  * Hold the line while another character receives visible credit.
* Around Level 12:
  * Negotiate between divided cultures without speaking only for his father.
* Around Level 15:
  * Build a voluntary alliance among factions that cannot be forced into unity.
* Late campaign:
  * Decide whether Vargun should remain bound to the pact.
  * Encounter an offer of permanent peace that eliminates future choice.

### Floyd GoldSeeker — Survival and Chosen Purpose

Possible pressure points:

* Does Floyd need a cosmic explanation for why he survived?
* How can he honor his crew without implying that their deaths were necessary?
* Can rescue, protection, remembrance, and chosen purpose be enough?

Optional spotlight opportunities:

* Level 2:
  * "You were not the only one buried. Dig."
* Levels 5-6:
  * Choose between trapped people and a powerful object or key evidence.
* Around Level 9 or 13:
  * Challenge the idea that magical resonance, divine attention, or survival measures a soul's worth.
* Late campaign:
  * Face possible histories in which the Glittervein crew survived.
  * Decide what the unexplained golden light means to him, if anything.
* Floyd's first complete arc should be capable of ending around Levels 5-6.
* If Floyd leaves, he may establish a rescue, excavation, surveying, or recovery organization that remains an ally.
* Do not define Floyd's god, domain, exact source of divine power, or custom divine mechanics.

## Villain Escalation

### Levels 1-4

* Opposition appears local: monsters, dragon exploitation, criminals, and cultists.
* Acererak is not visible.
* He benefits indirectly from instability.

### Levels 5-8

* Opposition expands to researchers, heist targets, false religious movements, and Fey rulers.
* Acererak purchases measurements and intercepts retuning purges.

### Levels 9-12

* Opposition becomes ideological and planar: psionic factions, planar criminals, centralizers, and divided communities.
* Acererak moves compressed dissonance through a multiversal network.
* Fragments of the growing tomb become visible.

### Levels 13-16

* Opposition concerns souls and failed restoration.
* Acererak's historical research and future signal become identifiable if he remains the approved antagonist.

### Level 17

* Opposition controls multiversal infrastructure.
* The future signal may be conclusively matched to Acererak.

### Levels 18-19

* Opposition centers on Chult, Omu, the Tomb, and the Soulmonger.
* Acererak attempts to transform his reservoir into a prototype Invariant.

### Level 20

* Opposition includes future Acererak, alternate manifestations, corrupted allies, and realities that prefer imposed certainty.
* Acererak attempts to collapse viable futures into the one in which he has already won.

## High-Level Design Rules

### Level 17

* Primary challenge: shared authority and systems management.
* Combat power alone cannot preserve every route.
* Player decisions determine which communities remain connected and which allies can act later.

### Level 18

* Primary challenge: leadership and delegation.
* Do not make ordinary jungle survival the main high-level challenge.
* Use settlement defense, expedition politics, conflicting factions, overlapping eras, impossible weather, and consequences for assigning allies to missions.

### Level 19

* Primary challenge: identity, temptation, and moral resource allocation.
* Do not rely only on instant-death traps.
* High-level failures may cost memories, allies, possible futures, access to restored histories, or control of battlefield conditions.

### Level 20

* Primary challenge: simultaneous multiversal objectives.
* The main party cannot personally defend every Anchor.
* Earlier allies and alternate characters act on their own fronts.
* The finale should involve:
  * Preserving the party's history.
  * Severing the backward signal.
  * Confronting Acererak's manifestation.
  * Protecting restored Anchors.
  * Making a final Concordance decision.
* Victory should reflect twenty levels of relationships and choices rather than initiative order alone.

## Master DM Rules

* Every module must work as a local adventure.
* Introduce no more than one major cosmic revelation per level.
* Aim for one primary spotlight, one secondary contribution, one relationship beat, and one consequential choice per module.
* Treat lore reveals and growth moments as different things.
* Retuned Anchors remain retuned.
* Acererak does not control every villain.
* Player repairs remain a net good.
* Include homeward echoes every three or four levels.
* Protect player ownership of unresolved backstory.
* Let Floyd's arc conclude early if the character leaves.
* Let alternate-character adventures remain consequential.
* Do not structure the campaign as collecting twenty identical objects.
* Build the Level 20 solution from:
  * Allies.
  * Routes.
  * Communities.
  * Histories.
  * Witnesses.
  * Character growth chosen by players.
  * Earlier irreversible choices.
